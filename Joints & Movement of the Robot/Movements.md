
## Robot Movement and Mathematical Representation

The robotic arm is a **5R serial manipulator**, meaning that all five joints are revolute joints and each joint contributes one rotational degree of freedom. The motion of the robot is therefore described in **joint space** by the joint-variable vector:

$$
\mathbf{q}=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_1 \\
q_2 \\
q_3 \\
q_4 \\
q_5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where each $q_i$ represents the angular position of the corresponding revolute joint.

The five joint axes defined in the kinematic model are:

- **Joint 1:** rotation about the $Z$-axis
- **Joint 2:** rotation about the $Y$-axis
- **Joint 3:** rotation about the $Y$-axis
- **Joint 4:** rotation about the $-Y$-axis
- **Joint 5:** rotation about the $X$-axis

Therefore, the overall movement of the robot is produced by the combined rotational motion of these five joints.

### Screw-Axis Representation

Each revolute joint is represented by a screw axis:

$$
S_i=
\begin{bmatrix}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \\
\mathbf{v}_i
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $\boldsymbol{\omega}_i$ represents the direction of the joint axis and $\mathbf{v}_i$ represents the linear component associated with that axis.

For a revolute joint:

$$
\mathbf{v}_i=
-\boldsymbol{\omega}_i \times \mathbf{q}_i
$$

where $\mathbf{q}_i$ is a point lying on the corresponding joint axis.

The five screw axes obtained from the robot geometry are:

$$
S_1 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
1 \\
0 \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
S_2 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
1 \\
0 \\
-5 \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
S_3 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
1 \\
0 \\
-21.5 \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
S_4 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
-1 \\
0 \\
39 \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
S_5 =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 \\
0 \\
0 \\
0 \\
39 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

These vectors mathematically describe the five independent rotational motions of the manipulator.

### Joint-Space Motion

As the robot moves, the joint angles vary with time:

$$
\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{q}(t)
$$

and the corresponding joint velocities are:

$$
\dot{\mathbf{q}} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot{q}_1 \\
\dot{q}_2 \\
\dot{q}_3 \\
\dot{q}_4 \\
\dot{q}_5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The motion of the end effector is produced by the combined effect of these joint motions.

Its instantaneous spatial velocity can be represented by:

$$
\mathbf{V}=
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{q})\dot{\mathbf{q}}
$$

where:

- $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{q})$ is the manipulator Jacobian
- $\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ is the vector of joint angular velocities
- $\mathbf{V}$ is the spatial velocity of the end effector

Thus, changing one or more joint angles changes the configuration of the complete serial chain and consequently changes the position and orientation of the end effector.

### Three-Dimensional Workspace

The robot operates in a **three-dimensional Cartesian workspace**. The joint coordinates used in the kinematic model are:

$$
\mathbf{q}_1=
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{q}_2 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
2.5 \\
5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{q}_3 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
3.5 \\
21.5
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{q}_4 =
\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
2.5 \\
39
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\mathbf{q}_5 =
\begin{bmatrix}
1.5 \\
0 \\
39
\end{bmatrix}
$$

with the dimensions expressed in centimetres.

These coordinates define the spatial arrangement of the joint axes. The main arm structure extends primarily along the $Z$-direction, while the offsets in $Y$ and the final wrist displacement in $X$ give the manipulator its three-dimensional geometry.

### Human-to-Robot Motion Mapping

The intended motion of the system is not based on a predefined geometric trajectory. Instead, the robot configuration changes according to the motion information obtained from the operator's arm.

The overall process can therefore be represented as:

$$
\text{Human Arm Motion}
\rightarrow
\text{Sensor Measurements}
\rightarrow
\text{Signal Processing}
\rightarrow
\mathbf{q}(t)
\rightarrow
\text{Robot Motion}
$$

The sensors provide measurements related to the operator's arm movement, which are processed and converted into the corresponding robotic joint commands. The resulting time-varying joint configuration,

$$
\mathbf{q}(t)
$$

then determines the robot's physical configuration.

The resulting end-effector motion can be represented as:

$$
\mathbf{T}(t)=
f\left(\mathbf{q}(t)\right)
$$

where $\mathbf{T}(t)$ represents the time-varying pose of the end effector and $f(\cdot)$ represents the robot's forward kinematic mapping.

Therefore, the robot follows a **spatial trajectory generated by the changing joint configuration**. The trajectory is not assumed to be a predefined straight line, circle, or other geometric curve; it is produced dynamically from the motion commands derived from the operator.

In summary:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Human Motion}
\rightarrow
\text{Sensors}
\rightarrow
\text{Processed Motion}
\rightarrow
\text{Joint Angles}
\rightarrow
\text{3D End-Effector Motion}
}
$$
