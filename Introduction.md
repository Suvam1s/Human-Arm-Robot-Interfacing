# Human Motion Controlled Robotic Arm
### A Biomedical Instrumentation and Embedded Systems Project

## Overview

This repository presents the complete design, development, and implementation of a **Human Motion Controlled Robotic Arm** developed as an interdisciplinary engineering project integrating **Biomedical Engineering, Embedded Systems, Analog Electronics, Signal Processing, Mechanical Design, and Control Systems**.

The objective of this project extends beyond simply building a robotic arm. It serves as a comprehensive demonstration of designing a complete sensing and actuation system—from raw biological motion acquisition to stable real-time robotic control. Every stage of the system, including sensing, analog front-end design, signal conditioning, mathematical analysis, firmware development, mechanical integration, and simulation, has been developed and documented.

Unlike conventional robotic arm projects that directly interface sensors with microcontrollers, this project focuses heavily on the **engineering of reliable sensor interfaces** capable of extracting usable information from extremely small and noisy analog signals.

---

## Project Objective

Human joint motion generates sensor outputs that are often nonlinear, low amplitude, highly susceptible to electrical noise, and unsuitable for direct acquisition by a microcontroller.

The primary objective of this work is therefore to design a complete acquisition and control pipeline capable of:

- Acquiring weak analog signals from wearable sensing systems.
- Eliminating unwanted electrical noise while preserving useful motion information.
- Amplifying very low-frequency signals without introducing instability or amplifier saturation.
- Designing analog front-end circuits that remain stable over varying operating conditions.
- Producing clean, repeatable analog outputs suitable for ADC acquisition.
- Translating processed sensor signals into smooth robotic joint movements in real time.

The resulting system demonstrates a complete sensor-to-actuator pipeline rather than simply servo motor control.

### Signal Processing

Particular emphasis has been placed on extracting meaningful information from extremely low-frequency analog signals generated by wearable sensors.

The signal processing pipeline focuses on:

- Removal of unwanted electrical noise
- Isolation of useful low-frequency motion components
- Gain optimization
- Prevention of amplifier saturation
- Stable amplification of weak sensor outputs
- Analog signal conditioning suitable for ADC acquisition
- Reliable sensor calibration
- Real-time signal interpretation

The objective is not simply amplification, but the generation of clean, repeatable, and stable sensor outputs that can be used for accurate robotic control without jitter or instability.

## Sensor Technologies

The robotic arm employs multiple sensing technologies, each selected according to the biomechanics of the corresponding human joint.

- MPU6050 Inertial Measurement Unit for shoulder orientation.
- Flex Sensor for elbow motion detection.
- Four-element strain gauge system for wrist bend estimation.

Each sensing system is paired with a dedicated analog conditioning stage before being interfaced with the ESP32.

---

## Control Strategy

The robotic arm follows a **master-slave teleoperation architecture**, where human motion directly determines robotic joint movement.

Instead of autonomous trajectory planning, the system performs:

- Sensor acquisition
- Analog signal conditioning
- Motion estimation
- Joint angle mapping
- Real-time servo actuation

This approach allows natural replication of human arm movement while maintaining stable operation and predictable joint behaviour.
